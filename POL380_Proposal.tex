\section{Proposed Topic}


Why do some democracies protect domestic producers more than others? I aim to study whether factors such as 
exports, unemployment rates, GDP, inflation rates, and the number of political parties causally shape how protectionist a country's trade policy is. Using a panel of many countries spanning several decades, I will estimate the effect of each factor using double machine learning (DML), which lets flexible learners such as random forests and neural networks estimate causal parameters in the presence of complex confounding variables \citep{DML}. 

\paragraph{Research Question:} \textit{Which domestic factors cause countries to adopt more protectionist trade policy, and how robust are these effects?} \\

\noindent This analysis aims to build upon prior analyses such as \textcolor{red}{\citep{Ehrlich2007} and similar, CITE}. Prior analyses have used linear regression to evaluate the causes of protectionism. I propose to use double machine learning to validate the results of \textcolor{red}{\citep{Ehrlich2007}  and similar, CITE}, creating a second avenue to test causal relationships in political science. \\

\subsection{Access to Protection: Domestic Institutions and Trade Policy in Democracies \citep{Ehrlich2007} } 

Sean Ehrlich's 2007 paper ``Access to Protection: Domestic Institutions and Trade Policy in Democracies'' tests various hypotheses regarding the causes of protectionism. He hypothesizes that more electoral districts, more political parties, low party discipline, bicameralism, presidentialism, and left-wing governments all lead towards increased protectionism. Using linear regression, Ehrlich determined that all hypotheses were true, except for the hypothesis that bicameralism causes increased protectionism, which was found false. \textcolor{red}{What about low party discipline? Not in class notes --> check paper itself.} Ehrlich also discusses access points, which refer to a policymaker susceptible to lobbying. Ehrlich argues that an increase in access points leads to an increase in protectionism.
